Privacy

Last updated 17 November 2026

Neurova is a student engineering project, built and run by one person. There are no advertisers, no analytics, and nothing here is sold or shared with anyone. This page says exactly what is collected, where it goes, and how to get it back or delete it.

No tracking of any kind. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no third-party cookies, no profiling, and nothing that follows you to other sites. None of that code exists in this project — not disabled, not configured off, simply never added.

Readings from the glove

The glove connects straight to your browser over USB or Bluetooth. Measurements travel from the board to the page in front of you and go nowhere else. If you are not signed in, closing or reloading the tab discards them and no copy exists anywhere.

If you are signed in, a record of each calibration and each contact test is saved to your account so the Data page can show a trend over time. That record holds the electrical measurement and nothing else about you:

  • Which kind of session it was — a calibration or a contact test
  • The baseline resistance in ohms, and whether it was measured fresh or restored from your account
  • For a contact test: the verdict, the count of good, poor and failed samples, and the average resistance
  • The two circuit constants of the board it was taken on, so a later resistor swap cannot silently corrupt the trend
  • The date and time it was taken

These are resistance values read through surface electrodes — a measure of how well the contacts are sitting against skin. There is no recording, no image, no location, and nothing that identifies you physically.

Your account, if you make one

Accounts exist for one reason: so a baseline and a session history can follow you between devices. You can sign in four ways — with Google, with an email address and a password, with a one-time link sent to your email, or as a guest.

Signing in normally

Stored against your account: your email address, the display name you choose, and your calibration baseline. If you set a password, it is handled entirely by Google's authentication service and stored only as a hash — it is never visible to this project or to me.

Signing in as a guest

Guest sign-in creates an account with no email address and no name attached. Sessions you log are stored against an anonymous identifier held in your browser, which means two things worth knowing: nobody, including me, can connect that data to you — and if you clear your browser's storage, the identifier is lost and that history can no longer be reached, including by you. Guest sign-in is meant for trying the app, not for keeping anything.

The contact form

When you send a message, three fields are submitted: your name, the return address you give, and the message itself. Each submission goes two places at once — to a database record and to a mailbox I read myself — because a single route can fail silently and a message that quietly vanishes is worse than one that bounces.

Your return address is used to reply to you and for nothing else. It is not added to a mailing list, because there is no mailing list. The form also carries a hidden field that only an automated script would fill in; when that happens the submission is discarded on the spot and neither stored nor emailed.

Stored on your device

A few small values are kept in your browser's own storage and are never transmitted:

  • Whether you chose the desktop or mobile version of the site
  • A flag noting that an account has been used in this browser, so the sign-in screen opens on the right form
  • Your email address, briefly, while a one-time sign-in link is being completed — then removed
  • A cached copy of the site's own files, so it still opens without a connection

Google's authentication service also keeps a sign-in token in browser storage while you are logged in. That is what signing out clears.

Services this site relies on

I run no servers of my own. Four outside services are involved, and because every one of them is contacted directly by your browser, each necessarily sees your IP address — that is simply how loading a web page works, and it is true of every site you visit.

  • GitHub Pages — hosts and serves the site's files.
  • Google Firebase — provides sign-in and the database holding accounts, baselines and session records.
  • Google Fonts — serves the two typefaces the site is set in.
  • Web3Forms — delivers contact form messages to my mailbox. Contacted only when you press send.

Each of these is a processor acting on this project's behalf, under its own privacy terms. None of them is given your data for their own purposes by me, and no data is ever sold, rented or traded.

Who can see your data

Database rules restrict every account's records so that only the signed-in owner of an account can read or write them. One person's baseline and history are not readable by another person using the app. As the project's administrator I can see the contents of the database, which means I can in principle open an account's session records; in practice I have no reason to and do not. Contact form messages I do read, since answering them is the point.

How long things are kept

Account data and session records are kept until you delete them — there is no automatic expiry, because a recovery trend is only useful over months. Contact form messages are kept so I can follow up on a conversation. Ask and I will delete yours.

Getting your data, and deleting it

Both live on the Account page, available to anyone signed in, with no need to ask me first:

  • Export my data downloads a JSON file holding your account details, your saved calibration baseline, and every session you have logged, each with its date.
  • Delete my account removes your session history, then your profile record, then the account itself, and signs you out. It cannot be undone.

Deletion is done in that order deliberately. A database of this kind does not remove records beneath a deleted account automatically — they survive their own parent — so the history is cleared first, while the account still exists and has permission to clear it. The result is that pressing the button genuinely empties the account rather than only detaching it.

Children

This project is not aimed at children and I do not knowingly collect anything from a child under 13. If you believe a child has created an account here, get in touch and I will remove it.

Security, honestly stated

Sign-in, password handling and database access control are all Google's implementation rather than mine, which is deliberate — they are far better at it than a solo project would be. Everything is served over HTTPS. That said, this is a student project without a security audit behind it, so the sensible advice is the ordinary advice: use a password you do not reuse elsewhere, and do not put anything in the contact form you would not want sitting in an inbox.

What this is not

Neurova Reach is a research and engineering project. It is not a medical device, it is not cleared or approved by any regulator, and nothing it reports should be used to make a decision about anyone's health or treatment. It measures electrode contact quality; interpreting recovery is a clinician's job.

Changes to this page

If what the project collects changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top is updated. The project's full history is public, so every revision of this page can be read in the repository it is served from.

Questions

Anything on this page, or any request about your own data, goes through the contact form on the site. Messages reach me directly and I answer them myself.
